              Safety tips

              Codes are secret

              No bank, company or government will ever ask for your PIN, password or a code sent to your phone.

              Knowing your name proves nothing

              Scammers buy leaked details. If someone calls from "your bank", hang up and call the number on your card.

              Gift cards are for gifts

              Anyone asking you to pay with gift cards, crypto or a transfer to a "safe account" is a scammer.

              Love doesn't ask for money

              If someone you've never met in person needs money, stop. Ask a friend before you send anything.

              Is that video call real?

              Ask them to turn their head fully sideways or wave a hand across their face. Never undress or send private photos on a call.

              A screenshot isn't payment

              Before you release goods, check the money in your own bank app. Receipts are easy to fake.

              Look twice at links

              "rnicrosoft" is not "microsoft". If a message rushes you to log in, open the app or website yourself instead.

              Already paid?

              Call your bank straight away and ask them to stop the payment. Then report it. Watch out for "recovery" offers: they're scams too.

              Scan before you scan

              Fake QR stickers get placed over real ones on parking meters, posters and menus. Scan them with TrueTick first.
